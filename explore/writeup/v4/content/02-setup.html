Setup

This section defines the models, the languages, the sentence representations, the two geometries, and an unbiased estimate of the squared distance between two languages.

Models. Qwen2.5-7B has 28 transformer blocks and a residual width of 3,584. Llama-3.1-8B has 32 blocks and a width of 4,096. We use the pretrained base checkpoints, not the instruction-tuned ones. Both run in bfloat16 without quantization.

Text. FLORES+ [9] gives the same sentences, professionally translated, in more than 200 languages. It has two disjoint halves: dev (997 sentences) and devtest (1,012 sentences).

Languages. We chose 34 languages to separate family, script and word order. They cover 10 families and 12 scripts. The 11 object-before-verb languages come from 5 families. Three pairs are one language in two scripts: Hindi and Urdu, Croatian and Serbian, and Chinese in simplified and traditional characters.

Sentence representations

Let h^\ell_t(x)\in\mathbb R^d be the residual stream at position t and layer \ell for an input x=(x_0,x_1,\dots,x_T). Every input starts with the same sink token x_0: the BOS token for Llama, and a newline for Qwen, which has no BOS token. In the middle layers the residual stream at position 0 has a norm much larger than at other positions. We therefore drop position 0 and average over the content positions:

\bar h^\ell(x)=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^{T}h^\ell_t(x).

Let x_{i,s} be sentence s in language i. On one half of FLORES with n sentences, the centroid and the offset of language i are

\mu_i^\ell=\frac1n\sum_{s=1}^{n}\bar h^\ell(x_{i,s}),\qquad a_i^\ell=\mu_i^\ell-\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mu_j^\ell,\qquad N=34.

All results in this article concern the 34 offsets and the distances between them. We write f_i for the mean number of content tokens per sentence of language i (its fertility).

Two geometries

A squared distance \lVert a\rVert_M^2=a^\top Ma needs a positive semi-definite matrix M. We use two, and every pre-registered claim must hold in at least one of them without a reversal in the other.

M_{\text{causal}}=\operatorname{diag}(\omega)\,\operatorname{Cov}_{y}\!\big[\gamma(y)\big]\,\operatorname{diag}(\omega),\qquad M_{\text{wh}}=\Big((1-\eta)\,W+\eta\,\tfrac{\operatorname{tr}W}{d}\,I\Big)^{-1},\quad \eta=\tfrac12 .
  • Causal geometry. \gamma(y) is the unembedding row of token y, and \omega is the weight vector of the final RMSNorm. The covariance runs over the real vocabulary: 151,665 tokens for Qwen, and the 128,000 non-special tokens for Llama. This is the causal inner product of Park et al. [5], under which the length of a direction measures how much it moves the logits. We apply it at every layer as an approximation in the style of the logit lens.
  • Whitened geometry. W is the pooled within-language covariance of the sentence representations on the dev half, W=\frac{1}{N(n-1)}\sum_{i}\sum_{s}\big(\bar h(x_{i,s})-\mu_i\big)\big(\bar h(x_{i,s})-\mu_i\big)^\top. Distances are then in units of the variation between sentences of one language. The shrinkage \eta keeps the inverse stable in 3,584 or 4,096 dimensions.

Unbiased squared distances

A squared distance between estimated offsets is biased upward by the sampling noise of the centroids. With \Sigma_i the covariance of the sentence representations of language i,

\mathbb E\,\lVert\hat a_i-\hat a_j\rVert_M^2\;\approx\;\lVert a_i-a_j\rVert_M^2+\frac{\operatorname{tr}\!\big(M(\Sigma_i+\Sigma_j)\big)}{n}.

Languages whose sentences vary more would therefore look farther apart. To remove this bias, we estimate each offset twice, on dev (\hat a^{(1)}) and on devtest (\hat a^{(2)}), and we cross the two estimates:

K_{ij}=\tfrac12\big(\hat a_i^{(1)\top}M\,\hat a_j^{(2)}+\hat a_j^{(1)\top}M\,\hat a_i^{(2)}\big),\qquad D^2_{ij}=K_{ii}+K_{jj}-2K_{ij}.

The two halves contain different sentences, so their sampling errors are independent and \mathbb E[K_{ij}]=a_i^\top Ma_j. Therefore D^2_{ij} is an unbiased estimate of the squared distance between the true offsets.

We estimate W on the dev half and treat M_{\text{wh}} as fixed in this argument.

The primary statistics use a pre-registered window \mathcal W of middle layers: layers 8–20 of Qwen and layers 9–23 of Llama. Both windows cover the same relative depth, 0.29 to 0.71. Over the window, we average the trace-normalized cross-half Gram matrices:

\bar K=\frac1{|\mathcal W|}\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal W}\frac{K^\ell}{\operatorname{tr}K^\ell},\qquad D^2_{ij}=\bar K_{ii}+\bar K_{jj}-2\bar K_{ij}.

Sampling noise in the centroids does not limit the results. In 50 sentence bootstrap resamples, which use the same sentence indices in every language, the 90% intervals of the primary gains have half-widths below 0.001. The number of languages limits the results.
