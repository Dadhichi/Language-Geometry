Where in depth

This section repeats the two marginal tests at every layer. The word-order split explains the most in the middle layers and almost nothing at the embedding. The family tree explains the most in the first and last layers. The pattern is the same in both models. These per-layer results are descriptive: the pre-registration lists them without a claim.

At each layer \ell we use the single-layer Gram matrix K^\ell/\operatorname{tr}K^\ell instead of the window mean, the same base terms, and 500 permutations per test. We compute four geometries (Euclidean, whitened with \eta=0.1 and \eta=0.5, and causal); the figure shows the two primary ones. The horizontal scale is relative depth, \ell divided by the number of blocks, so that the two models align.

The two profiles suggest that the two structures have different sources. The first and last layers read and write tokens. Related languages share word forms, so a family signal at these layers can come from the form of the tokens. But the base terms already remove the unigram token distance. In addition, the character-overlap control of the next section changes the per-layer family-tree gain by at most 0.002 (Qwen) and 0.008 (Llama) at any layer. A source in word form at the edges therefore remains possible, but we do not show it.

Word order is not a property of any single token. It is a property of how tokens are arranged. It appears in the layers that earlier work describes as a shared, largely language-neutral space [4][7]. It also nearly disappears at the last layer, where the model prepares the next token.
