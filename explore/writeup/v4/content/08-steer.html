Is the axis used?

A structure in the representations can describe the data without the model reading it. This section tests use. We add a word-order direction to the residual stream of Qwen2.5-7B and measure a change in behaviour that only word order can explain. The pre-registered test passes: the direction shifts the model's preference between the two orders of the same words, in all 8 test languages.

The word-order direction

At layer 14 we regress the dev-half offsets on language properties. It is one ordinary least-squares regression with all 3,584 coordinates as responses:

a_j=\beta_0+\beta_{\text{OV}}\,\mathbb 1_{\text{OV}}(j)+\beta_{\text{IE}}\,\mathbb 1_{\text{IE}}(j)+\sum_{\sigma\in\{\text{Latin},\,\text{Cyrillic},\,\text{Arabic},\,\text{Devanagari}\}}\beta_\sigma\,\mathbb 1_\sigma(j)+\beta_f\log f_j+e_j .

The coefficient \beta_{\text{OV}}\in\mathbb R^{3584} is a direction in the residual stream. It is the difference between object-before-verb languages and the others, with Indo-European membership, script and fertility held fixed. For each test language i, we fit the regression on the other 33 languages and call the result \beta_{\text{OV}}^{(-i)}. The direction is therefore never fitted on the language that it steers. At layer 14 its norm is between 11.6 and 12.1, and its cosine with \beta_{\text{IE}} is between −0.03 and +0.02.

Before any intervention, we check where each test language falls on its own held-out direction:

\mathrm{proj}_i=\frac{\big\langle a_i,\ \beta^{(-i)}_{\text{OV}}\big\rangle}{\lVert\beta^{(-i)}_{\text{OV}}\rVert^2}.

On this scale, the mean projection of the OV languages is between +0.50 and +0.53. The mean projection of the other languages is between −0.24 and −0.25. The ranges come from the 8 regressions, one for each left-out test language.

Minimal pairs

From Universal Dependencies treebanks [12] in 8 languages, we took 999 real sentences in which a verb and its object are adjacent. For each sentence we built a variant with the two parts swapped. The two variants contain exactly the same tokens. The languages are English, French, Russian and Chinese (verb before object), Hindi, Turkish and Japanese (object before verb), and German (no dominant order). We keep a sentence if:

  • the verb is the first clause-level verb with an object dependent;
  • the object subtree is one contiguous span of at most 6 words without punctuation;
  • the object span is adjacent to the verb with its auxiliaries and particles;
  • neither span starts the sentence, the sentence has 5 to 40 words, and it has no multi-word tokens.

The same detokenizer rebuilds both variants, so their formatting cannot differ.

Intervention and test statistic

We add a multiple of a direction v to the output of block 14, at every position including the sink:

h^{14}_t\;\leftarrow\;h^{14}_t+k\,v,\qquad k\in\{{-2},{-1},{+1},{+2}\}.

The strength k is in units of \lVert\beta^{(-i)}_{\text{OV}}\rVert. For a minimal pair x=(x^{\text{OV}},x^{\text{VO}}) we score both variants under the steered model:

m_k(x)=\log P_k\big(x^{\text{OV}}\big)-\log P_k\big(x^{\text{VO}}\big),\qquad \log P(x)=\sum_{t\ge1}\log P(x_t\mid x_{<t}),\qquad \Delta_k(x)=m_k(x)-m_0(x).

Both variants contain the same tokens. Suppose that a push only makes some words, or the language as a whole, more probable. Then it changes both variants by similar amounts, and the change largely cancels in m. A change in the preferred order does not cancel. The pre-registered statistic is the slope over k=\pm2, computed per language and averaged over the 8 languages with equal weight:

b=\frac1{8}\sum_{i}\frac{\sum_{k=\pm2}k\,\bar\Delta^{(i)}_k}{\sum_{k=\pm2}k^2}=\frac18\sum_i\frac{\bar\Delta^{(i)}_{+2}-\bar\Delta^{(i)}_{-2}}{4},

where \bar\Delta^{(i)}_k is the mean over the pairs of language i. Any push of this size disturbs the model. The null distribution therefore uses 32 random directions of the same norm, drawn in the same space of language differences:

r=\lVert\beta^{(-i)}_{\text{OV}}\rVert\,\frac{U\epsilon}{\lVert U\epsilon\rVert},\qquad \epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I),

with U an orthonormal basis of the span of the 33 centred offsets. The control direction is \beta_{\text{IE}}^{(-i)}, rescaled to the same norm.

Claim rule (pre-registered)

The word-order direction is used if b_{\text{OV}}>0, b_{\text{OV}} exceeds all 32 random slopes (empirical p = 1/33 = 0.03), and z=(b_{\text{OV}}-\overline{b_r})/\mathrm{sd}(b_r)>2.33. Layer 14 is primary. Layers 8 and 20, strengths ±1, the control slope, the sign count over languages and a pair-bootstrap interval are secondary.

Result

The pre-registered claim holds. The slope of the word-order direction is b_{\text{OV}}={+1.52} nats per unit of k (95% pair-bootstrap interval 1.40 to 1.65). The 32 random directions give −0.05 ± 0.32, with a maximum of +1.16. Thus z = 4.99, and the empirical p = 0.03 is the smallest possible with 32 directions. The slope is positive in all 8 languages.

The secondary results agree. The Indo-European direction gives −0.25, inside the range of the random slopes. The dose response is monotone at layers 8, 14 and 20. Over all four strengths, the slopes are 1.06, 1.43 and 1.00 nats per unit of k. The per-language slopes range from 0.88 (Japanese) to 2.96 (Chinese).

Asymmetry and cost (exploratory)

The following analyses were not pre-registered.

  • Asymmetry. A push toward the order that a language does not have moves it far. A push toward the order that it already has moves it little. Chinese moves from −14.1 to −3.5 nats at k = +2, and only to −15.3 at k = −2. Hindi moves from +9.0 to +3.4 at k = −2, and only to +8.9 at k = +2. This is the pattern we expect if the model reads the direction to decide word order and is already confident in the familiar case.
  • Cost. The intervention is not surgical. At k = +2 it lowers the log-probability of the attested sentence by 33.8 nats on average. Random directions of the same norm lower it by 17.1 nats on average (range 1.9 to 95.9 nats).
  • Flips. Unsteered, the model prefers the attested order in 96% of pairs. At k = +2, 9.3% of pairs change their preferred variant toward OV; at k = −2, 10.0% change toward VO. The Indo-European direction at k = +2 also changes 8.8% of pairs, toward VO. Flip counts are therefore not specific; the pre-registered slope is.

This test measures a preference between two strings that we wrote. The model never has to produce either string. The next section asks whether the same push changes the order that the model writes.
