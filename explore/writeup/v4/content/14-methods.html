Methods and pre-registration

This section gives the pre-registrations and the procedures that a reader needs to reproduce every test. Each study directory under explore/ holds its pre-registration (PREREG.md), the frozen analysis code, the job script and the result files. We wrote and validated each confirmatory test on simulated data, and committed it, before we extracted or analysed the data that it uses. Analyses added later are in separate scripts with "exploratory" in their names.

Extraction

  • Forward hooks on the embedding module and on every decoder block capture the residual stream. We do not use the last hidden state of the Hugging Face output, because it already includes the final norm.
  • Pooling happens inside each hook, so no full sequence of activations is kept across layers. We store the pooled activations in float16. The models run in bfloat16 without quantization.
  • Batches are formed by length and limited by a budget of padded tokens. A batch that runs out of memory is split.
  • Every input starts with the sink token (BOS for Llama, a newline for Qwen), and pooling drops position 0.
  • Fertility f_i is the mean number of content tokens per sentence over both halves. The token distance uses the token-frequency distributions of the dev and devtest text, with each model's own tokenizer.

Geometries, distances and the split regression

  • Causal geometry. The covariance of the unembedding rows uses the normalization N_V-1 over the real vocabulary (rows with an index below 151,665 for Qwen and below 128,000 for Llama). \omega is the weight of the final RMSNorm.
  • Whitened geometry. W is computed per layer on the dev half. The per-layer profiles also use the shrinkage \eta=0.1 and the Euclidean inner product.
  • Bootstrap. 50 resamples of sentences with multinomial weights. The same sentence indices apply to every language, and the window mean is recomputed for each resample.
  • Rescaled pooling. Each sentence vector is multiplied by \sqrt{T_{i,s}/\bar T}, where T_{i,s} is its number of content tokens and \bar T the mean over all languages and both halves. The tests then use 2,000 permutations.
  • Fit. Non-negative least squares on the 561 values of D^2_{ij}. The columns are the 34 star indicators, the 4 script splits, the two covariates divided by their standard deviations, and the splits under test. R^2=1-\mathrm{SSE}/\mathrm{SST}.
  • Permutations. For the family tree, a random permutation of the 34 language labels maps every split of \mathcal T to a new split of the same size and the same nesting. For a single split, the null draws random subsets of the same size. A draw counts as at least as large if G_b\ge G_{\text{obs}}-10^{-12}.
  • Same-language test. Residuals of the fit with the star and the two covariates only. Each pair receives its percentile rank among the 561 residuals (0 = closest). The statistic is the mean rank of the three pairs; the p-value counts random triples of pairs with a mean rank at least as small.
  • Power. Before data collection we simulated Brownian motion on the Glottolog tree with realistic leaf lengths and script structure. With 34 languages and internal branches that carry 10% of the distance, the family-tree test has a power of 80% to 86%; with the 12 pilot languages, 18% to 42%.
  • Exploratory robustness. 2,000 permutations. Geography is the great-circle distance between the WALS coordinates of two languages, added as a covariate.

Typology and lexical controls

  • The WALS values come from the WALS data in CLDF format. Uncoded values take the value of the genus: Kazakh for 83A, 85A and 86A, Azerbaijani for 85A, and Maltese for 87A.
  • Character overlap: transliteration with unidecode, lower case, sets of character trigrams, Jaccard index per parallel sentence, mean over the dev and devtest sentences.
  • ASJP: the 40-item lists. The word lists were fixed in advance; Serbian uses the Serbo-Croatian list, both Chinese scripts use the Mandarin list, and Filipino uses the Tagalog list. For synonyms, LDN takes the minimum over the forms.
  • Tests A1 and A2 use random subsets of 11 and 12 languages; B1 and B2 use the leaf-permutation null. 10,000 permutations each.

Steering with minimal pairs

  • Directions. Ordinary least squares on the dev centroid offsets in the raw coordinates of the residual stream, at layers 8, 14 and 20, once for each held-out test language. The Indo-European direction is rescaled to \lVert\beta_{\text{OV}}\rVert. The 32 random directions are Gaussian vectors in the span of the 33 centred offsets, normalized and scaled to \lVert\beta_{\text{OV}}\rVert, with a fixed seed.
  • Pairs. The test sets of the Universal Dependencies treebanks English EWT, German GSD, French GSD, Russian SynTagRus, Hindi HDTB, Turkish IMST, Japanese GSD and Chinese GSD, at most 150 pairs per language.
  • Scoring. The sum of the log-probabilities of the content tokens given the sink. The steering vector is added to the output of the block at every position, including the sink.
  • Conditions. Unsteered; the word-order and Indo-European directions at layers 8, 14 and 20 with k\in\{\pm1,\pm2\}; the 32 random directions at layer 14 with k=\pm2.
  • Statistics. \mathrm{sd}(b_r) uses the normalization n-1. The empirical p-value is (1+\#\{r:b_r\ge b_{\text{OV}}\})/33. The 95% interval of b_{\text{OV}} comes from 5,000 bootstrap resamples of pairs within each language.

Free generation

  • Prompts. A seeded shuffle of the 1,012 devtest indices; the first study takes the first 150, and the confirmatory study takes the next 150 that the first study did not use. A prompt keeps the first 40% of the words (at least 3 words), or for Chinese and Japanese the first 40% of the characters (at least 6).
  • Language identification. langid.py [14], restricted to the study languages; a continuation shorter than 3 characters counts as no language.
  • Parsing. Stanza with tokenization, multi-word-token expansion where the language has it, part-of-speech tagging, lemmatization and dependency parsing, applied to prompt and continuation together. A word counts as generated if it starts after the end of the prompt.
  • Exploratory statistics. The prompt bootstrap uses 2,000 resamples of the 150 sentence indices. The re-parse by object type ran on the unsteered condition, the word-order and Indo-European directions at ±2 and all 48 random conditions.
  • Confirmatory run. A debug run on 2 prompts per language with 1 random direction tested the pipeline first; its numbers are not used. The frozen analysis code (analysis_gen2.py) contains the synthetic check of power and false-positive rate and produced results_gen2.json.
  • Confirmatory, descriptive additions. The prompt bootstrap of the pooled slopes (bootstrap_gen2.py, not pre-registered) draws the 150 sentences with replacement 2,000 times. Each draw applies to every language and condition and keeps the inclusion rule. The destination languages of Polish and Croatian continuations come from the language-identifier labels of the generated text.

Within-language direction

  • States. The 999 minimal pairs of the preference test, with the sink token prepended. Mean pooling over the content tokens with the sink dropped, at every layer, in the same way as for the language centroids (pairstates.py).
  • Estimator. Weighted least squares per layer and coordinate, with an intercept (d_W), the sign term s_p (d_U) and weights 1/n_{\ell(p)}. The synthetic check used planted vectors and the real pair counts. Split-half reliability uses a random half of the pairs in each language.
  • Alignment. \beta_{\text{OV}} and \beta_{\text{IE}} from the regression of the preference test, fitted on all 34 languages. Null: 10,000 Gaussian vectors in an orthonormal basis of the span of the 34 centred dev centroids, normalized. p=(1+\#\{\text{null}\ge\cos\})/10{,}001.
  • Generation. The 1,350 prompts of the confirmatory study, greedy decoding of 40 tokens, block 14, every position. Nine conditions: unsteered, d_W at \pm1,\pm2,\pm3, and \beta_{\text{OV}} at \pm2. The random null comes from the confirmatory study; its unsteered and \beta_{\text{OV}} continuations are identical to this run.
  • Retention test. The against sign uses the unsteered noun-object share (above 0.5 means object-first; all objects if a language has no noun pair). Two-sided sign test; languages with equal rates are left out.
  • Debug run. 3 pairs and 2 prompts per language tested the pipeline first; its numbers are not used.
  • Llama replication. The frozen estimator and alignment test of the Qwen study, applied to Llama-3.1-8B pair states (pairstates_l.py, analysis_wl.py). \beta_{\text{OV}} and \beta_{\text{IE}} come from the 34 Llama dev centroids; the null uses their span. Primary layer 16 of 32. Extraction only, without steering; a debug run on 3 pairs per language came first.

Token-level study

  • Each token state is projected onto the 11-dimensional span of the language offsets (dev centroids) and onto the top 117 principal components of the token states orthogonal to that span. The tests use the span coordinates.
  • The tag-conditioned log-likelihoods cover the first 24 content tokens of every sentence (283,900 positions). Ambiguous positions have \max_j\pi_t(j)<0.9.
  • The baseline for token identity uses the layer-0 embedding of the current token, projected to the span, plus a one-hot code of the position.
  • The code-switched sequences use the states at 8 layers and the log-likelihoods under the tags of A and B. The lag is censored at the length of the B segment.

Compute

The GPU jobs ran on Google Colab, on L4 GPUs. Extraction for one model and 34 languages takes about 30 minutes; the whole project used about 10 L4 GPU-hours. The confirmatory free-generation run used 2.76 Colab compute units, the within-language study 0.69, and its Llama replication about 0.2.
