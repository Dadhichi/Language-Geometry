Testing for a tree and an axis

This section turns the map into two pre-registered tests. Random evolution along a tree predicts that squared distances are a sum of terms, one for each split of the languages. We use this to measure how much the family tree and the word-order split explain. Both structures pass, each beyond the other, in both geometries and in both models.

From a tree to a regression on splits

Suppose that the offset of each language is a sum of independent random steps along the branches from a root r to that language:

a_i=\sum_{e\,\in\,\mathrm{path}(r,\,i)}\xi_e,\qquad \mathbb E[\xi_e]=0,\quad \mathbb E\big[\xi_e^\top M\xi_e\big]=\sigma_e^2,\quad \xi_e\ \text{independent}.

In the difference of two offsets, the steps on the common part of the two paths cancel. The other steps add in quadrature:

\mathbb E\,\lVert a_i-a_j\rVert_M^2=\sum_{e\,\in\,\mathrm{path}(i,\,j)}\sigma_e^2 .

Each internal branch e divides the languages into those below it and the rest. This is a split S_e. The branch adds \sigma_e^2 to exactly the pairs that its split separates. Each leaf branch adds a term for one language. With the indicator \delta_S(i,j)=\mathbb 1\big[\lvert S\cap\{i,j\}\rvert=1\big], a tree with splits \mathcal S predicts

D^2_{ij}=u_i+u_j+\sum_{S\in\mathcal S}\theta_S\,\delta_S(i,j)+\phi^\top c_{ij}+\epsilon_{ij},\qquad u,\ \theta,\ \phi\ \ge 0 .

The terms u_i form the star. The covariates c_{ij} remove known surface effects. We fit all coefficients by non-negative least squares over the 561 pairs of languages.

Base terms and gain

The base terms are the terms that every test keeps fixed:

  • the star, u_i for each of the 34 languages;
  • one split for each script that more than one language uses: Latin, Cyrillic, Arabic and Devanagari;
  • the token distance 1-\sum_y\min\big(p_i(y),p_j(y)\big), where p_i is the frequency distribution of the model's tokens in the FLORES text of language i;
  • the fertility difference \lvert\log f_i-\log f_j\rvert.

We divide each covariate column by its standard deviation. In Qwen, the base terms explain 85% (causal) and 92% (whitened) of the variation of D^2. We score a set of splits \mathcal S by its gain, the fraction of the remaining squared error that it removes:

G(\mathcal S\mid\mathcal A)=1-\frac{\mathrm{SSE}\big(\mathcal A\cup\mathcal S\big)}{\mathrm{SSE}\big(\mathcal A\big)} .

Here \mathcal A is the base terms plus any splits that we hold fixed, and SSE is the residual sum of squares of the non-negative fit. We write G(\mathcal S) when \mathcal A is the base terms alone.

The two structures and their null distributions

  • Family tree, \mathcal T: the 20 internal splits of the Glottolog tree [11] restricted to our languages. Eleven splits are in Indo-European: Indo-European itself; Germanic; West Germanic (English, German, Dutch); Romance; Ibero-Romance (Spanish, Portuguese); Slavic; East Slavic (Russian, Ukrainian); South Slavic (Croatian, Serbian); Indo-Iranian; Indo-Aryan (Hindi, Urdu, Marathi); Hindi–Urdu. Nine are in other families: Semitic; Arabic–Maltese; Turkic; Oghuz (Turkish, Azerbaijani); Finnic; Chinese; Austronesian; Austroasiatic; Dravidian. The null distribution permutes the leaf labels and keeps the shape of the tree.
  • Word-order split, S_{\text{OV}}: the 11 languages with object-before-verb order in WALS feature 83A [10], against the other 23. WALS does not code Kazakh; we assign it to the OV group because all coded Turkic languages are OV. The null distribution draws random sets of 11 languages.

Each test uses B=10^4 random draws. The p-value is p=\big(1+\#\{b:G_b\ge G_{\text{obs}}\}\big)/(1+B), so the smallest possible value is 0.0001.

Conditional tests and claim rules

The two structures overlap, because the OV group consists mostly of complete subtrees. In simulation, a planted family tree alone makes the marginal word-order test reject, and a planted word-order split alone makes the marginal family-tree test reject. Each claim therefore also needs a conditional test, which holds the other structure fixed: G(\mathcal T\mid S_{\text{OV}}) and G(S_{\text{OV}}\mid\mathcal T). The conditional tests use the same null distributions.

Claim rules (pre-registered)

Family-tree claim: G(\mathcal T) and G(\mathcal T\mid S_{\text{OV}}) both have p < 0.0125 in the same geometry.

Word-order claim: G(S_{\text{OV}}) and G(S_{\text{OV}}\mid\mathcal T) both have p < 0.0125 in the same geometry.

A claim must hold in at least one geometry and must not reverse in the other. The threshold 0.0125 is a Bonferroni correction of 0.05 over {family tree, word order} × {causal, whitened}.

Same-language test (secondary, α = 0.05): Hindi–Urdu, Croatian–Serbian and the two Chinese scripts are closer than predicted. The prediction uses the star, the token distance and the fertility difference, without script terms. The statistic is the mean percentile rank of their three residuals among the 561 pairs. The null distribution draws random sets of three pairs.

We checked this design on simulated distances before any data existed. The simulation used Brownian motion on the real tree, a realistic star with one high-fertility outlier, nuisance script structure, and 34 languages. The table gives the rejection rates at α = 0.05.

Both claims hold in both geometries and both models. The family tree and the word-order split each explain the distances beyond the other and beyond script, shared tokens and fertility (all p ≤ 0.0007). The Llama-3.1-8B replication was pre-registered with no change except the layer window and the model-specific inputs.

The conditional gains are close to the marginal gains, so neither structure is a proxy for the other. The effects are larger in Llama.

The same-language test also passes in all four analyses (p ≤ 0.0078). In the whitened geometry of both models, the three same-language pairs are among the closest 0.7% of the 561 pairs. In the causal geometry of Llama, they are among the closest 1.1%. In the causal geometry of Qwen they rank lower, between the 7th and the 21st percentile. The distances therefore put one language in two scripts closer together than its token statistics predict.
