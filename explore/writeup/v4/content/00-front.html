The Word-Order Axis

Two multilingual language models arrange 34 languages by descent and by word order. The two structures occupy different depths. In Qwen, steering along the word-order structure changes which order the model prefers and, in German and Russian, the order in which it writes.

Summary

We study how two multilingual language models, Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3.1-8B, place languages relative to one another in their residual stream. Each model reads the same 2,009 sentences in 34 languages (FLORES+ translations). We represent each language by the mean of its sentence representations, and we analyse the distances between these means. shows the main structure in one picture.

Linguists classify languages by descent, in family trees, and by type, for example by the order of verb and object. We ask whether either classification appears in the distances beyond script, shared tokens and sentence length, and whether the model uses it. The main results follow; the introduction defines the evidence labels.

  • Two structures, each beyond the other. The distances contain the Glottolog family tree and a split of the 11 object-before-verb languages from the rest, each beyond the other, in both models (all p ≤ 0.0007). Pre-registered.
  • Different depths. Word order is strongest between 25% and 71% of the depth. The family tree is strongest in the first and last layers. Descriptive.
  • The axis is head direction. Object–verb order and adposition order each explain the distances beyond the other. The family tree is not explained by shared vocabulary. Pre-registered.
  • The model uses the axis. A direction estimated from 33 other languages shifts Qwen's preference between the two orders of 999 real sentences, in all 8 test languages (z = 4.99). Pre-registered.
  • It also changes what Qwen writes. A first test could not be evaluated. On fresh prompts, noun objects before the verb then fell from 66% to 8% in German and rose from 4% to 58% in Russian (z = 7.83). In Llama this test fails (z = 1.86). Pre-registered: replication holds in Qwen, fails in Llama.
  • Other languages: open. In Qwen, the test on four new languages was not evaluable. In Llama, these four languages reordered (z = 6.27). Qwen: not evaluable. Llama: secondary, no claim.
  • A word-order feature inside each language lies along the axis. Cosine +0.23 in Qwen and +0.32 in Llama. In Qwen, steering along it reorders German and Dutch and keeps the language (98% against 76%); in Llama it misses the threshold. Pre-registered: alignment holds in both; steering only in Qwen.
  • Language identity switches fast. It is graded when the evidence is ambiguous, but at a code switch it moves within about one token, faster than a Bayesian observer. Pre-registered: graded belief holds; Bayesian switching does not.
